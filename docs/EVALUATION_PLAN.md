# Pathway learning — evaluation plan

## Product questions

1. Can learners and families describe an aspiration with more clarity than a job title or examination name?
2. Does the pathway make approved curriculum more relevant without reducing foundational breadth?
3. Do learners build stronger mastery, reasoning, writing, problem-solving, and practical capability?
4. Do authentic projects and service experiences improve commitment and transfer?
5. Which leading indicators predict examination, employer, further-study, or civic outcomes?
6. Can a gram panchayat or institution adopt the playbook without reproducing the first team's hidden advantages?
7. Does the model improve opportunity without increasing pressure, premature tracking, or exclusion?

## Measurement layers

- **Learner:** mastery, reasoning, writing, problem solving, aspiration clarity, agency, wellbeing, and persistence.
- **Pathway:** progression against required capabilities, quality of work, exposure to real contexts, and performance on the relevant gate.
- **Institution:** teacher time, implementation fidelity, content reuse, cost per learner, source integrity, and local adaptation.
- **System:** transition outcomes, equity across subgroups, portability across locations, public value, and capital mobilised for wider access.

## Longitudinal design

A full class-6-to-career outcome may take 12–15 years. The first deployment should establish baseline measures, consented longitudinal tracking, comparison groups where appropriate, and a clear distinction between family advantage and pathway contribution. Leading indicators must be registered as hypotheses and validated against later outcomes before they are used for high-stakes decisions.

## Stop conditions

Pause expansion if the system narrows a learner's options without informed consent, turns uncertainty into a fixed label, increases distress or pressure, exposes sensitive data, performs worse for a subgroup, or claims pathway impact without separating it from family resources, school quality, coaching, or selection effects.
