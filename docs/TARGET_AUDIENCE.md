# Pathway learning — target audience

## Initial learning cohort

The first cohort is a hypothesis-driven segment, not a claim about the eventual beneficiary population.

Urban Indian parents who:

- work in technology or adjacent knowledge roles;
- have experienced a mismatch between education and work;
- are actively exploring alternative schooling, homeschooling, or mastery-based learning;
- can provide time, devices, mentors, and access to projects;
- want credible examinations, progression, and future optionality.

They are useful first participants because they may tolerate experimentation and provide unusually rich feedback. Their privilege must be measured so it is not confused with product impact.

## Public-interest expansion

The expansion sequence moves through learning communities, gram panchayats, districts, states, and national platforms. Each expansion requires local research rather than treating a poorer context as a smaller version of the initial cohort.

## Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | Need | Trust question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learner | Relevant learning and a credible future path | Can I change direction without losing years? |
| Parent | Confidence that the path is safe and worthwhile | Will this preserve future options? |
| Teacher / mentor | Tools that extend human guidance | Does the system respect my judgment? |
| Gram panchayat / district | A workable local implementation | Can this function with our language, staffing, and connectivity? |
| State / national system | Interoperable, measurable public infrastructure | Can we adopt it without depending on one vendor? |
| Funder / philanthropist | Evidence of public value and additionality | Does capital create durable capability for underserved learners? |
