# Product judgment

This document is the review standard for the education work in this repository. It exists because a convincing vision can become vague very quickly once it is translated into product language.

## The thing we are trying to change

Education is treated as a long sequence of subjects and certificates, while the reason for pursuing it is usually a better life. The connection between the two is often postponed until an examination, a job search, or the first years of work.

The proposition is to make that connection visible earlier. A learner should be able to ask: what kind of work or contribution might I want to make, what foundations does that require, what can I practise now, what hard gates exist, and what other routes preserve the same purpose?

This is a proposition to test. It is not a claim that an eleven-year-old can be assigned a profession or that conventional subjects have no value.

## Product truth

The repository can say with confidence:

- Education-to-work alignment is weak in India, and graduate employability is uneven. The evidence ledger records the sources and their limits.
- Certificates sometimes represent real hard gates: a selective examination, a regulated licence, a formal eligibility rule, or a trusted employer/institution assessment.
- Certificates are weak when they are used as a generic proxy for capability that can be demonstrated directly.
- EkStep’s work is mission-first, ecosystem-oriented, and concerned with reusable public infrastructure and diffusion. The relevant question is therefore not “what app should be built?” but “what implementation can be made right, reusable, and useful beyond one team?”
- A full class-6-to-adult outcome may take 12–15 years to observe.

The repository cannot yet say:

- that the proposed model produces better careers;
- that urban technology-working parents are a quantified market of a particular size;
- that Bihar families specifically prefer UPSC above other aspirations at a measured rate;
- that early aspiration clarity predicts examination success or a good life;
- that evidence from affluent families will transfer automatically to poorer communities.

Those are research questions or hypotheses, not copy claims.

## The human decision

The first prototype must help a learner and parent make one decision:

> What kind of contribution are we seriously willing to explore, and what would we need to learn, practise, experience, and keep open in order to explore it responsibly?

The prototype does not need to simulate the entire sixteen-year education system. It needs to make that decision concrete enough for a family, mentor, or reviewer to challenge it.

## Non-negotiable distinctions

### Foundations and direction

Common foundations remain necessary. Direction becomes more explicit after the foundations are strong enough and the learner’s interests are observable. Orientation is revisable; it is not early tracking.

### Purpose and gate

UPSC, NEET, IIT-JEE, licensing, and employer assessments can be hard gates. They are tests of access to a particular route, not the whole meaning of public service, medicine, engineering, or a good life.

### Primary route and adjacent route

Every pathway needs alternatives that preserve the underlying purpose. A learner interested in mechanical engineering should not choose an unrelated branch only because of institutional prestige. A learner interested in public service should see state services, public health, education, rural development, research, and local governance as real routes where relevant.

### Evidence and aspiration

An aspiration can guide what to investigate. It cannot be presented as a prediction. Leading indicators are useful only when registered as hypotheses and checked against later outcomes.

### First adopters and final beneficiaries

Resource-secure urban families are a possible first learning cohort because they may have more room to experiment. They are not the moral endpoint. If the model works for them, they may also become advocates, mentors, connectors, and sources of capital for wider access. Transfer still has to be demonstrated in each new context.

## Language we accept

Use:

- “what kind of work do you want to explore?”
- “what problem or group of people do you want to serve?”
- “what does this lesson make possible?”
- “what can you demonstrate?”
- “what is the relevant hard gate?”
- “what other routes preserve the same purpose?”
- “this is an early hypothesis”

Do not use:

- “discover your destiny”;
- “AI chooses the right career”;
- “personalised education at scale” without naming the human decision and evidence;
- “guaranteed placement”, “guaranteed success”, or “future-proof child”;
- “disrupt education”;
- “replace school”, “replace teachers”, or “remove foundations”;
- “poor families will adopt once the rich prove it” as if adoption were automatic;
- “leading indicators” without saying what they are indicators of and how they will be validated.

## Review questions

Before accepting a document, screen, or prototype, ask:

1. What is the one human decision or handoff being improved?
2. Is the claim supported, inferred, or explicitly a hypothesis?
3. Does the work preserve common foundations, learner agency, alternatives, and real hard gates?
4. Does it explain how knowledge becomes practice, experience, assessment, and contribution?
5. Is the first adopter described as a test context and future constituency, not as a shortcut to social impact?
6. Does the seed-to-forest story name what must be learned at each level?
7. Would a parent, learner, teacher, or public official understand what happens next?
8. Is the language something a thoughtful person would actually say aloud?

## What the prototype must prove

The next prototype should prove one bounded thing: that a family can move from a broad aspiration or exam label to a more specific, revisable contribution hypothesis and a credible first pathway of learning, practice, experience, assessment, and alternatives.

It should not pretend to prove the 15-year outcome. It should make the next research question visible.
