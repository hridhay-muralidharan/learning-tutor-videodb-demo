# Evidence ledger: education, work, and pathway learning

This is a living research ledger. Claims are marked as supported, qualified, or untested so the product narrative does not outrun the evidence.

## Supported or strongly evidenced

### Education-to-work alignment is weak in India

The World Bank describes a loose connection between higher education and the labor market. Its review reports concerning graduate outcomes and India Skills Report employability scores in 2021 of 46.82% for engineering, 30.34% for science, 42.72% for arts, and 40.30% for commerce. It also reports that more than half of students and graduates lack demanded cognitive and soft skills.

Source: [World Bank, Employment and employability](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099080503142332315/pdf/P1747790711faf048085a70993d530a04de.pdf)

### Degree inflation is a real hiring problem

Harvard Business School and the Burning Glass Institute documented employers adding degree requirements to jobs without a corresponding change in the work. Their research examined 26 million job postings and identified millions of middle-skill jobs affected by degree inflation.

Sources: [Harvard Business School](https://www.hbs.edu/news/releases/degree-inflation-us-competetiveness), [Harvard/Burning Glass skills-based hiring](https://pw.hks.harvard.edu/post/skills-based-hiring-the-long-road-from-pronouncements-to-practice)

### Skills-first hiring is growing but remains incomplete

Indeed reported that 52% of US job postings did not mention a formal education requirement in January 2024, up from 48% in 2019. Harvard and Burning Glass caution that changing job descriptions has often produced much smaller changes in actual hiring. This supports the need for trusted evidence infrastructure, not just removing degree language.

Source: [Indeed Hiring Lab](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2024/02/27/educational-requirements-job-postings/)

### Middle-stage differentiation is an existing curricular transition

India's official curricular framing places classes 6–8 in the Middle Stage, where subject teachers, abstract concepts, and interdisciplinary exploration become more prominent. Classes 9–12 add deeper subject focus, critical thinking, flexibility, and student choice. This supports using the middle stage for structured exploration, but does not by itself prove that children should lock into a profession at age 11.

Sources: [NCERT stage framework](https://neec.ncert.gov.in/en/about/), [DIKSHA curriculum structure](https://diksha.gov.in/exploreasparent/)

## Qualified or currently untested

### Affluent, educated families are likely early adopters

The broader diffusion literature supports the direction of this hypothesis. Rogers' synthesis associates earlier adoption with greater formal education, higher socioeconomic status, more resources, and movement toward higher status. Research on technology adoption similarly finds that information access, income, education, and risk capacity affect early adoption. This supports an initial experiment with resource-secure families, but it does not establish the best segment for this specific education product.

Sources: [Rogers diffusion synthesis](https://laulima.hawaii.edu/access/content/group/MAN.1326.201213/Readings/Rogers_Ch7.pdf), [innovation adoption and socioeconomic status review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2214629617300993)

### Urban Indian alternative-learning interest exists, but the segment is not quantified well

Available reporting identifies small, urban clusters of homeschooling and unschooling activity, especially around Bengaluru and Pune, but does not provide a representative national estimate or a reliable count of technology-worker parents seeking aspiration-led education. This segment should be treated as a discovery and pilot cohort, not yet as a proven market size.

Sources: [Bangalore Mirror on urban unschooling](https://bangaloremirror.indiatimes.com/bangalore/cover-story/unschool-is-so-cool-parents-look-at-alternative-forms-of-learning/articleshow/61800104.cms), [Times of India on Indian homeschooling](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/life-style/parenting/moments/why-are-some-indian-parents-saying-no-to-conventional-schools-meet-the-families-choosing-homeschooling-and-other-alternatives/articleshow/132166586.cms)

### The diffusion sequence can reduce uncertainty over time

Diffusion research models adoption as a process in which early users generate information that reduces uncertainty for later users. The education-specific claim—that validated leading indicators can shorten later deployment cycles—is a product hypothesis that requires longitudinal validation. It is plausible as an implementation-learning mechanism, but cannot be presented as an already proven outcome.

Source: [Pricing, referrals, and diffusion on networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.06544)

### “Most school knowledge has no relevance”

Not established as a general claim. The stronger, testable claim is that curricula often fail to make relevance visible, provide applied practice, or connect learning to later pathways.

### “UPSC is a common aspiration in rural and semi-urban Bihar”

Plausible, but not yet established with a representative Bihar-specific dataset. A small government youth survey in Udham Singh Nagar found 71% interest in government jobs, including UPSC and banking, but it is not evidence about Bihar.

Source: [District youth aspirational survey](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s33cf2559725a9fdfa602ec8c887440f32/uploads/2025/06/202506261101200207.pdf)

Required next evidence: Bihar youth aspiration surveys, government-job preference data, Patna coaching enrolment and migration studies, and interviews segmented by gender, caste, rurality, income, and education.

### “Early pathway orientation increases eventual success”

This is a product hypothesis, not an established conclusion. It should be tested against risks of premature tracking, family pressure, unequal access to information, and aspiration lock-in. The design should preserve common foundations, exploration, reorientation, and adjacent pathways.

### “A five-year product-management apprenticeship beats a degree”

Cannot be claimed without controlling for selection effects and comparing actual hiring outcomes. The defensible claim is that demonstrated, role-relevant work may be stronger evidence than a degree alone for many jobs, provided employers can verify its quality.

## Research questions for the next iteration

- Which occupations genuinely require a degree or license, and which use it mainly as a screening proxy?
- Which capabilities predict entry-level and mid-career performance in each pathway?
- How early can a learner make an informed aspiration choice?
- What experiences and information reduce premature lock-in?
- Which parts of UPSC preparation can be distributed across school and college without narrowing general capability?
- Can a pathway system improve outcomes without increasing pressure on children or reproducing existing class advantage?
- What evidence will an institution, employer, parent, and learner each trust?
